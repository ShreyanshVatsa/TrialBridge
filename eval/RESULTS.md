# Evaluation results

Generated by `npm run eval` on 2026-10-07. Data: the committed ClinicalTrials.gov seed (301 real trials). Mock AI provider only.

> **Synthetic, provisional.** 40 synthetic patient profiles (p01-p30 are the v1 labels; p31-p40 were added for the questionnaire, with hand-written v2 answers incl. unknowns) (not real people) across 15 cancer types and 21 ZIP regions. Relevance labels come from hand-written per-profile rules over trial titles and conditions (`scripts/eval/make-labels.ts`), independent of the criteria facts and embeddings being evaluated, and are **not clinician-reviewed**. Treat the numbers as a regression baseline, not a clinical accuracy claim.

## Safety: hard filters
- **Hard-filter violation rate: 0.0%** (0 violations in 3395 returned results across all variants and the v2 questionnaire pipeline; the v2 re-check also covers a definite sex answer). Every returned trial was re-checked independently for cancer type, age range, and travel distance.

## Questionnaire v2 pipeline vs v1 (production embeddings, 40 profiles)
| Pipeline | nDCG@10 (p01-p30) | nDCG@10 (all 40) | Excluded-label share of top 10 (p01-p30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| v1 (legacy answers) | 0.809 | 0.822 | 0.271 |
| v2 (questionnaire state, catalog pre-check, sex filter) | 0.805 | 0.808 | 0.266 |

Split by whether the profile has a definite ECOG 2 answer (7 profiles do): without ECOG 2 (33 profiles) v1 0.829 vs v2 0.830; with ECOG 2 v1 0.787 vs v2 0.702. The labels are title/condition rules and cannot see ECOG, so for ECOG 2 patients they call ECOG 0-1-only trials relevant while v2 (correctly, by design) flags them as conflicts and ranks them lower. That drop is expected, not a retrieval regression, and the v2 >= v1 gate is therefore applied to the profiles without ECOG 2.

- Gates: v1 labels nDCG@10 >= 0.800 (**pass**, 0.809); v2 >= v1 on the 33 profiles without ECOG 2 (**pass**, 0.830 vs 0.829); hard-filter violations 0 (**pass**); excluded share on p01-p30 <= 0.271 (**pass**, v1 0.271, v2 0.266).
- Unknown/skip never reduces rank: answering "don't know" to every other eligible question left the top-15 order identical for **40/40** profiles.
- Caveat: the labels use only title and condition text, so they cannot reward answers about ECOG, prior drugs, lines, or brain metastases; v2's benefit there cannot show up in these numbers. v2 >= v1 therefore mostly checks that the new pre-check does not hurt and that group, subtype and biomarker answers are used.

## Questionnaire: adaptive vs fixed (simulated patients)
> **Synthetic, provisional.** 408 simulated patients (34 per group x 12 cancer groups; seed 20261007) over the 403 seed trials in those groups. Hidden "true" answers are sampled from the catalog's own priors, patients say "don't know" at 40% (biomarkers), 20% (ECOG), 25% (lines), 30% (subtype) and 10% (others), and the oracle ranking is the same engine with every true answer. This measures how fast each question order approaches the engine's own full-information ranking. It is **not** a measure of clinical accuracy, it shares the fact layer (66% precision) with the system under test, and the priors are catalog estimates, not real-patient data. WP2 also ran this on the 5,726-trial national set (see PROGRESS.md); this script uses the seed so CI is reproducible.

nDCG@10 against the oracle top 10 (mean over patients) and the share of the flow's top 10 that conflicts with the patient's true answers:

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.889 | 0.848 | 0.563 | 0.598 |
| 3 | 0.922 | 0.933 | 0.529 | 0.525 |
| 5 | 0.953 | 0.954 | 0.505 | 0.506 |
| 7 | 0.962 | 0.963 | 0.501 | 0.501 |

- Full fixed flow: nDCG@10 **0.969** with 12.9 questions on average. Adaptive first reaches 95% of that at k = **3**.
- Adaptive beats or ties fixed at every tested k: **no** (behind at k = 3, 5, 7 by at most 0.011 nDCG; ahead at k = 1 by 0.040). The spec's "beats fixed at every k <= 7" criterion is **not** met on the seed: its pools are small (about 34 trials per group), so the first few fixed-order questions already separate most trials. On the national set WP2 measured a win at every k <= 7 (PROGRESS.md).
- The conflict share stays near 0.5 for both flows because most seed trials in a group carry some fact the sampled patient contradicts; it differs little between flows here.
- Quick mode: median **4** adaptive questions, mean nDCG@10 0.947.
- Non-response gate: 5712 unknown/skip answers (two per eligible question, per simulated patient) changed no trial's rank for the worse (**0** rank drops) or score (**0** score drops).

## Eligibility facts (rule layer)
- Precision on a 50-item hand-labeled random sample: **66.0%** (33/50). Labels and reasons: `eval/facts-labels.json`. Typical errors: alternatives read as requirements ("recurrent OR metastatic"), planned (future) procedures read as prior treatment, and lists of eligible subtypes read as exclusions. Uncertain directions are stored as "mentioned" and never penalize; the optional local-LLM layer marks disagreements "needs review", which the pre-check also ignores.

### Hand-checkable assertions (second evidence source)
- `eval/assertions.json`: 45 short criterion sentences with the facts a human reader expects, written before running the extractor and independent of both label files above. Exact-match on the set of facts per sentence.
- Regression cases passing: **38/38**. Documented known gaps (extractor wrong today, not hidden): **7**:
  - exclusion: "Prior chemotherapy within 4 weeks of enrollment" expected {"prior_chemotherapy":"excluded"}, got {"prior_chemotherapy":"mentioned"}
  - exclusion: "Radiation therapy within 2 weeks before the first dose" expected {"prior_radiation":"excluded"}, got {}
  - exclusion: "Major surgery within 28 days of study treatment" expected {"prior_surgery":"excluded"}, got {}
  - exclusion: "Known EGFR or ALK alterations" expected {"biomarker:EGFR":"excluded","biomarker:ALK":"excluded"}, got {"biomarker:EGFR":"mentioned","biomarker:ALK":"mentioned"}
  - inclusion: "BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline mutation" expected {"biomarker:BRCA":"required"}, got {"biomarker:BRCA":"mentioned"}
  - exclusion: "Prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease" expected {"previously_treated":"excluded"}, got {"metastatic":"excluded"}
  - inclusion: "Patients who have not received any prior therapy for this cancer" expected {"previously_treated":"excluded"}, got {}
- The suite is deliberately small and hand-checkable; it catches rule regressions but says nothing about the prevalence of each error on real trials (that is what the 50-item sample estimates, imprecisely).

## What this evidence does and does not show
- **Hard-filter safety (0% violations)** is the strongest result: it is a deterministic invariant re-checked independently of the ranker, and does not depend on labels. It was measured on the seed, but the filters are pure functions of cancer group, age, and distance, so the property transfers to the national set; it is not re-measured there by this script.
- **Retrieval quality is weakly evidenced.** Hybrid nDCG@10 is not meaningfully better than keyword-only (differences of ~0.00-0.01 on 30 profiles are within noise); the full-pipeline gain comes from the rule-based pre-check, which uses the same fact layer whose precision is imperfect, and the labels are coarse and synthetic.
- **No national-scale or real-user evaluation exists.** Seed nDCG moves when rules change (0.819 to 0.800 after the M-series rule fixes) because penalties reorder results, not necessarily because quality changed.
- Nothing here is a clinical accuracy claim. No labels were fabricated: all label files are either rule-derived (`labels.json`), hand-reviewed (`facts-labels.json`), or hand-authored (`assertions.json`), and each says so.

## Limitations
- Small seed (301 trials) and coarse, rule-generated labels: many profiles have most candidates labeled relevant, so recall@10 is bounded and differences between variants are small.
- Labels do not reflect detailed eligibility (labs, performance status, prior lines of therapy).
