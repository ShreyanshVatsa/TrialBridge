# Evaluation results

Generated by `npm run eval` on 2026-10-12. Mock AI provider only. All profiles are synthetic; nothing here is a clinical accuracy claim.

> **How to read this file.** Section 1 is the headline: the production ranker against a random baseline and a BM25 baseline, on the national data, with 95% bootstrap confidence intervals. Section 2 checks the hard filters against an independent oracle computed from raw ClinicalTrials.gov fields. Section 3 (seed, 301 trials) is a **smoke test** that guards against regressions. The relevance labels in sections 1 and 3 are regexes over trial titles and conditions, not clinician judgements, and BM25 reads the same text, so they flatter keyword matching. Graded, quote-backed judgements live in the vignette benchmark (`eval/gold/`, `npm run eval:vignettes`) and the TREC Clinical Trials cancer subset (`eval/trec/`); those, not this file, are the evidence for retrieval quality.

## 2. Safety: hard filters checked by an independent oracle
The app's filters (cancer group, age, distance, definite sex) read derived card fields. Checking a result with the same filter functions can never fail, so the old "0 violations" line (reproduced in the last bullet) is true by construction and is not evidence. `scripts/eval/oracle.ts` instead re-derives each decision from the **raw CT.gov record** with separate code: its own cancer-group term lists over conditions, keywords and titles; its own parser of the raw minimum/maximum age strings; and its own haversine over the registry's facility coordinates of recruiting U.S. sites. Distance is counted with 25 miles of slack, because the app measures to ZIP centroids and the registry gives facility coordinates (the strict count is shown too). A "group" flag means the raw text names a different cancer than the profile's; it can also be the oracle's term list being narrower than the registry's wording, so read flagged samples before concluding anything.
- **Seed** (305 raw records), over all variants and the v2 pipeline: **66 of 3395 results (1.9%): group 66, age 0, sex 0, distance 0 (strict, no slack: 0); distance not checkable for 7 (raw record has no coordinates); 0 had no raw record**. ## Questionnaire: adaptive vs fixed (simulated patients)
> **Synthetic, provisional.** 408 simulated patients (34 per group x 12 cancer groups; seed 20261007). Hidden "true" answers are sampled from the catalog's own priors, patients say "don't know" at 40% (biomarkers), 20% (ECOG), 25% (lines), 30% (subtype) and 10% (others), and the oracle ranking is the same engine with every true answer. This measures how fast each question order approaches the engine's own full-information ranking. It is **not** a measure of clinical accuracy, it shares the fact layer (66% precision) with the system under test, and the priors are catalog estimates, not real-patient data. The seed run (403 trials) is CI-reproducible; the national run uses the shards in public/data when present. The engine picks questions by information gain plus the expected change of the top 10 (DECISIONS.md); the engine constants were tuned on a different patient draw (seed 777, six of the twelve groups), so these numbers (seed 20261007, all groups) are held-out.

## Eligibility facts (rule layer)
- Precision on a 50-item hand-labeled random sample: **66.0%** (33/50). Labels and reasons: `eval/facts-labels.json`. Typical errors: alternatives read as requirements ("recurrent OR metastatic"), planned (future) procedures read as prior treatment, and lists of eligible subtypes read as exclusions. Uncertain directions are stored as "mentioned" and never penalize; the optional local-LLM layer marks disagreements "needs review", which the pre-check also ignores.

### Hand-checkable assertions (second evidence source)
- `eval/assertions.json`: 45 short criterion sentences with the facts a human reader expects, written before running the extractor and independent of both label files above. Exact-match on the set of facts per sentence.
- Regression cases passing: **38/38**. Documented known gaps (extractor wrong today, not hidden): **7**:
  - exclusion: "Prior chemotherapy within 4 weeks of enrollment" expected {"prior_chemotherapy":"excluded"}, got {"prior_chemotherapy":"mentioned"}
  - exclusion: "Radiation therapy within 2 weeks before the first dose" expected {"prior_radiation":"excluded"}, got {}
  - exclusion: "Major surgery within 28 days of study treatment" expected {"prior_surgery":"excluded"}, got {}
  - exclusion: "Known EGFR or ALK alterations" expected {"biomarker:EGFR":"excluded","biomarker:ALK":"excluded"}, got {"biomarker:EGFR":"mentioned","biomarker:ALK":"mentioned"}
  - inclusion: "BRCA1 or BRCA2 germline mutation" expected {"biomarker:BRCA":"required"}, got {"biomarker:BRCA":"mentioned"}
  - exclusion: "Prior systemic therapy for metastatic disease" expected {"previously_treated":"excluded"}, got {"metastatic":"excluded"}
  - inclusion: "Patients who have not received any prior therapy for this cancer" expected {"previously_treated":"excluded"}, got {}
- The suite is deliberately small and hand-checkable; it catches rule regressions but says nothing about the prevalence of each error on real trials (that is what the 50-item sample estimates, imprecisely).

## What this evidence does and does not show
- **Hard-filter safety** is checked against an independent oracle built from raw CT.gov fields (section 2), on the seed and, when the raw national pages are cached, at national scale. The earlier "0% violations" claim was circular and is withdrawn. The oracle is a second opinion with its own limits (term lists, 25 mile distance slack, registry coordinates), not ground truth; any flagged samples should be read by a person.
- **Retrieval quality is measured against baselines, not asserted.** Sections 1 and 3 report random and BM25 baselines with bootstrap CIs. The labels are title/condition regexes that BM25 also reads, so a pipeline-over-BM25 gain here is conservative on semantics and a pipeline-below-BM25 result is not alarming by itself; neither is a statement about patients. Differences whose interval includes 0 are not distinguishable.
- **What would be real evidence** is graded, quote-backed judgement: the synthetic-vignette benchmark (`eval/gold/`) and the TREC Clinical Trials cancer subset (`eval/trec/`). Neither is summarised here.
- **No real-user evaluation exists.** Nothing here is a clinical accuracy claim. Label files are rule-derived (`labels.json`), hand-reviewed (`facts-labels.json`), or hand-authored (`assertions.json`), and each says so.

## Limitations
- Profiles are synthetic and few (40); intervals are over profiles, so they are wide for subgroup claims.
- Labels do not reflect detailed eligibility (labs, performance status, prior lines of therapy); on easy profiles most candidates are labelled relevant, which raises the random baseline.
- The oracle's cancer-group term lists were written independently but by the same team; a shared blind spot is possible.
